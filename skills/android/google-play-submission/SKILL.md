# Google Play submission

## Handoff and scope

Start with [release-preflight](../../universal/release/release-preflight/SKILL.md) for version, privacy, and policy readiness; [release-artifacts](../../universal/release/release-artifacts/SKILL.md) for the signed AAB and its identifiers; and [store-submission-content](../../universal/release/store-submission-content/SKILL.md) for truthful localized release notes, listing assets, and the submission record. This skill covers **Play Console actions and observed states**, not building/signing a bundle or inventing store copy. Obtain authorized Console access, the intended app/package, artifact/versionCode, target track, countries/regions, and release owner. Never claim an upload, review, approval, or launch without corresponding Console evidence.

## Select the route and complete Console setup

1. In the correct Play Console app, choose **Internal testing**, **Closed testing**, **Open testing**, or **Production** according to the release plan. Confirm the account has the needed testing/production permissions, app setup is complete, and the chosen track is available. For accounts subject to additional testing requirements before production access, run the required closed test, apply from the Dashboard, and **wait for production access to be granted**; an internal test or an application for access is not production approval. Check the [current account-specific requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465?hl=en) and live Console rather than assuming fixed tester counts or eligibility.
2. Complete the [App content declarations](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859455?hl=en) and required store setup for the actual app: privacy policy, ads, target audience/content, content rating, Data safety, restricted permissions, and any other declarations shown in Console. Reconcile answers with the submitted app, SDKs, services, and listing; do not guess or mark a requirement complete merely because a form was saved. Check current Console and policy requirements for this app rather than hardcoding platform or policy thresholds.
3. Where sign-in, membership, subscription, location, QR code, MFA, or other restriction gates content, supply **Sign-in details/App access** in App content: reusable working reviewer credentials, access paths, roles, any necessary test data and English instructions that allow reviewers to reach every restricted feature. Make access independent of expiring OTPs or location, provide any required bypass or static resource, and test against the release's real backend. Keep credentials in approved Console fields, not public release notes or logs. See Google's [review access requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/15748846?hl=en).

## Prepare and send the release

1. On the intended track, create or edit the release; upload the signed **AAB** or select the intended processed bundle from the library. Wait for Play's processing/validation, inspect reported errors and warnings, and verify package, versionCode, version name, included/excluded bundles, release notes, and the selected track against the artifact handoff. Resolve blocking errors. Verify target countries/regions and tester eligibility before making a testing or production release available; check Console's current rollout controls.
2. Inspect the release preview/confirmation and **Publishing overview** together: releases, App content, store listing, and country changes may all be pending. Saving a draft or uploading an AAB is **not** submitting it. If changes appear under **Changes ready to send for review** / **Changes not yet sent for review**, confirm the exact intended set and select **Send for review**. Do not accidentally send unrelated changes; hold or remove them using Console's supported controls. Where the Console instead presents a **Publish** or rollout action for a change that does not require review, understand its immediate availability implications before confirming. A click is not evidence of review approval; check resulting status.
3. First production publication has **no staged rollout percentage**: it targets users in the selected countries/regions after applicable review and publishing. Percentage-based [staged rollouts](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6346149?hl=en) apply to eligible **updates**, not first publication. [Managed publishing](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859654?hl=en) also requires an app already available on Play; for eligible updates, enable it before submission if approval must be held for a deliberate launch. Check which changes it does *not* hold back. Without it, reviewed changes generally publish automatically after approval. For an approved managed update, wait until the intended set is under **Changes ready to publish** and none of it remains in review, then explicitly **Publish changes**.

## Diagnose state, rejection, and availability

- **Draft / ready to send:** bundle or changes prepared, not under review. **In review:** submitted changes are being evaluated, not approved or live. **Approved / ready to publish:** applicable managed changes passed review but are still held; not live until published. **Available:** Console shows the expected release active on the intended track, with the intended country targeting and rollout reach. Keep app, update, and individual item statuses distinct: an older production version can remain live while its replacement is in review or rejected. See Google's [publishing status definitions](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859751?hl=en).
- If rejected, read the actual enforcement notice, [Policy status](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9842754?hl=en), affected version/track, and cited policy or reviewer-access problem. Record precisely what the Console reports, not a speculative cause. Fix the actual issue in the app, backend, reviewer credentials, declarations, or listing; if the binary changes, produce a new signed AAB with a valid new versionCode via the artifact skill. For a policy-violating bundle, inspect **all affected testing and production tracks** and exclude/deactivate that bundle as directed by the notice before resubmitting a compliant release; don't assume a production-only correction covers active tests. Recheck the affected declarations and release, then **Send for review** again from Publishing overview where required. If the decision itself is demonstrably mistaken, follow the notice's [appeal process](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/2477981?hl=en) with specific evidence instead of misrepresenting an unchanged app as fixed. Never equate appeal submission with reversal or resubmission with approval.
- After publication, inspect the release's track/status and versionCode in **Latest releases and bundles**, its country/region targeting and (for an eligible staged update) live rollout percentage. Verify Play Store availability using an eligible account/device in a selected region; account/track eligibility and propagation can affect what is visible. Record observed Console state, release/build IDs, target scope, submission and publication evidence, and remaining uncertainty in the version-scoped store record; don't manufacture timestamps or screenshots. If live visibility is unverified, report **published in Console, storefront availability unverified**, not a completed launch.

Official workflow: [Prepare and roll out a release](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859348?hl=en) and [Control when changes are reviewed and published](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859654?hl=en).
